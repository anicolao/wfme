# The War for Middle-earth

## A strategy game of counsel, journeys, and dominion

One to four great commanders struggle to shape the fate of Middle-earth. At one or two players, automated Rivals fill the contested board. Each player sends Agents across the map, seeks standing among four powerful factions, gathers resources, and builds a personal deck of allies, places, and stratagems. At the close of every round, armies converge on one decisive battle.

This is a game of timing rather than conquest by attrition. A commander who spends everything on the current war may lose the wider campaign. A commander who builds a perfect court may discover that somebody else has already won the field.

## Objective

Renown measures a commander's claim upon the age. At the end of a round, the game ends if any player has at least 10 Renown or if the Battle deck is empty. The player with the most Renown wins.

Renown comes from faction standing and alliances, battle victories and matching battle standards, select cards, and secret endgame Fate cards.

## The central loop

Each round reveals one Battle card. Players then alternate Agent turns, playing a card to send an Agent to a matching board space, or take a Reveal turn and commit the rest of their hand to recruitment and combat strength. The battle is resolved, unattended resource spaces accumulate, Agents return, and first player passes clockwise.

Every deck card has two lives:

- its Journey box sends an Agent and creates an immediate effect;
- its Muster box contributes Influence, swords, or another effect when kept for the Reveal turn.

Using a powerful travel card early may forfeit its swords later. Saving it may surrender the exact space an opponent needs.

## Resources

- **Gold** buys institutional power, pays retainers, and funds War Efforts.
- **Mithril** is rare strategic wealth used for exceptional musters, craft, and the Deep Roads.
- **Provisions** pay for difficult journeys through the wild.
- **Influence** exists only during a Reveal turn and buys cards from the Chronicle Row.
- **Renown** is the victory score.

## Armies

Each player's cubes are **Companies** worth 2 Strength in battle. Their identity follows their commander: levies, riders, rangers, orcs, Uruk-hai, Easterlings, and other hosts all use the same common rule.

**Ents** are great units worth 3 Strength. A player must first earn an Ent-draught token before summoning them. Ents enter battle immediately, never wait in a garrison, and double the reward their owner receives from that battle. The intact Dam bars Ents from battles at Minas Tirith, Osgiliath, and Edoras.

## The board

Six icons govern travel:

- Shadow Hosts
- Dwarven Holds
- Elven Realms
- Wild Kindreds
- White Council
- Strongholds
- Roads

The first four are factions with influence tracks. The remaining three organize the shared political and geographic spaces.

The White Council offers permanent offices and political leverage. Strongholds offer troops, cards, and control of critical locations. Roads and deep places generate Mithril, accept War Efforts, and lead toward dangerous but powerful destinations.

## Signature systems

### Scouts

Each player has three Scouts. A Scout occupies an observation post connecting one or more spaces. Recall it to infiltrate an occupied connected space or to draw a card after placing an Agent at a connected space. Some cards use Scouts as costs or grant the Scout icon as a flexible travel permission.

### The Dam

The Dam begins intact and protects the three settled critical locations. A player with sufficient standing among the Wild Kindreds can breach it at Fangorn Moot; rare card effects can also do so. The breach is permanent and allows Ents into any battle.

### Battle standards

Each Battle and Objective card bears one of three standards: White Tree, Horse, or Star. Winning a Battle card creates a pair with another card of the same standard to score 1 Renown. The pair is then turned face down.

### War Efforts

The optional War Effort module adds public contracts. Players accept a War Effort, satisfy its condition, and claim its reward. This creates alternate tempo goals without becoming a separate scoring game.

### Commanders

Each commander has one always-available ability and one Ring ability triggered by playing the shared **Token of Command** starting card on an Agent turn. Starting decks are otherwise identical in the baseline game. This makes balance and onboarding cleaner while preserving leader identity.

## Tone

The setting is presented as a mythic counterfactual: rival leaders compete to determine the shape of an age, and faction influence represents favor, leverage, knowledge, fear, or command—not necessarily friendship. The game does not attempt to reproduce a single canonical campaign.
