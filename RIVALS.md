# Rivals — Solo and Two-player Rules

Automated Rivals occupy destinations, compete for faction Alliances and Banners, and contest Battles. They deliberately do not simulate deck-building, Scouts, Fate hands, or Ents. These rules favor fast, legible turns over human-like optimization.

## 1. Additional components

- 22 Rival action cards, one for each destination listed in section 8
- 4 Rival profile cards
- one unused player color and normal player components per Rival

Text slips or a numbered table and a twenty-two-card ordinary deck are sufficient for prototyping.

## 2. Player counts

### Solo

One human faces two Rivals. The human wins only by finishing with strictly more Renown than each Rival.

### Two players

Two humans share one Rival. The Rival contests the game but cannot win. At Endgame compare only the two humans using the normal tiebreakers. The Rival can hold Renown, Alliances, Objectives, and Banners, but reaching 10 Renown does not trigger Endgame.

## 3. Rival setup

After normal setup, for each Rival:

1. Choose an unused color and take its 3 Agents, 12 Companies, 4 faction markers, 3 Banners, Renown marker, and combat marker.
2. Put 3 Companies in its garrison and 9 in its supply.
3. Put two Agents in its ready area and its third beside the board.
4. Put one marker at 0 on every faction track and its Renown marker at 0.
5. Give it 1 Provision, 0 Gold, and 0 Mithril.
6. Deal it a face-up Objective.
7. Deal it a Rival profile. With two Rivals, use different profiles.

Shuffle the 22 Rival action cards into one shared deck and leave room for a face-up discard pile.

### Turn order

In solo play, seat the two Rivals after the human. The First Player marker rotates among all three seats normally.

In two-player play, seat the Rival between the humans. Randomly choose a human first player. The marker alternates between the humans each round; the Rival always acts between them.

Rivals count as players and opponents for occupancy, faction Alliances, control, Battle ranks, and effects that refer to all players. They are never valid targets for effects concerning hands, cards, decks, Fate, Scouts, Council seats, or War Efforts.

## 4. Rival profiles

A profile lists faction priority from highest to lowest. When a Rival may gain standing with any faction, choose the first listed faction in which it does not already have standing 6.

| Profile | Faction priority | Captain round | Ability |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| The Black Captain | Shadow, Wild, Dwarven, Elven | 5 | +1 Battle Strength while participating |
| The Mountain King | Dwarven, Elven, Wild, Shadow | 4 | The first Mithril gained each round is doubled |
| The Far-seer | Elven, Shadow, Dwarven, Wild | 4 | The first non-battle action each round that gains Gold gains 1 additional Gold |
| The Border Marshal | Wild, Dwarven, Elven, Shadow | 5 | The first battle action each round deploys 1 extra Company from the garrison if possible |

At the start of the listed round, place the Rival's third Agent in its ready area. It has three Agent turns that round and thereafter. This does not occupy Captain of the Host.

## 5. Rival Agent turns

If a Rival has a ready Agent, it takes an Agent turn. It never takes a Reveal turn.

1. Reveal Rival action cards until one names an unoccupied destination. Discard skipped cards.
2. Put one ready Rival Agent on that destination.
3. Ignore the destination's normal costs, requirements, and effects. Resolve only the action card's Rival effect.
4. If it is a battle action, deploy as described below.

If the action deck empties, shuffle its discard pile immediately and continue. A Rival never uses a Scout to infiltrate and never occupies an observation post. Human players may infiltrate a space occupied by a Rival normally.

When the Rival has no ready Agent, simply skip it each time its seat is reached. After every human has Revealed and every Rival has used all ready Agents, Player Turns end.

## 6. Rival recruitment and Battle

Recruit Rival Companies normally from supply to garrison. On a battle action:

1. Recruit the number printed by the action, if any.
2. Deploy all Companies recruited by that action.
3. Deploy up to two additional Companies from its garrison.
4. The Border Marshal may deploy its one extra Company on its first battle action of the round.

A Rival does not reveal swords or play Combat Fate. If it participates, add an escalation bonus to its unit Strength: Age I `+0`, Age II `+1`, Age III `+2`. Add profile bonuses after escalation.

Rivals receive ranked rewards, win Battle cards, control locations, and pair Standards normally. They never double rewards because they never summon Ents. Convert unusable reward elements as follows:

- draw a Fate card → gain 1 Gold;
- draw a deck card → gain 1 Gold;
- place or recall a Scout → gain 1 Gold;
- acquire a Chronicle card or gain Influence → gain 2 Gold;
- take or complete a War Effort → gain 2 Gold;
- gain an Ent-draught token or summon an Ent → recruit 2 Companies per Ent instead.

Ignore trashing, discarding, and Council-seat effects. All other resources, standing, recruitment, control, and Renown resolve normally.

Rivals do not act in the Combat Fate window. Resolve Battle cleanup normally.

## 7. Rival round phases

At Round Start, a Rival that controls the named critical location deploys its defensive Company normally. Rivals do not draw hands.

During Riches, an Agent belonging to a Rival prevents accumulation normally.

During Recall, return Rival Agents to their ready areas. Unlock a third Agent if the next round is that profile's Captain round.

In solo play, either Rival reaching 10 Renown triggers Endgame normally. Rivals do not play Endgame Fate. A tie between the human and any Rival is a human loss.

## 9. Difficulty

Choose one setting before solo play:

| Level | Adjustment |
| --- | --- |
| Wayfarer | Rivals begin with no Companies in their garrisons; the human draws 1 Fate during setup. |
| Captain | No adjustment. |
| Nazgûl | Rivals each begin with 1 Renown and +1 Gold. |
| Dark Lord | Use Nazgûl setup; all Rival profiles gain another +1 Battle Strength while participating. |

For a gentler two-player game, begin the Rival with no garrison. For a harsher game, apply the Dark Lord Battle bonus.

## 10. Rival precedence

When several legal choices remain, a Rival chooses in this order:

1. the choice that gains the most Renown immediately;
2. the choice that takes or preserves a faction Alliance;
3. the choice that gains control of a critical location;
4. its profile's faction priority;
5. most Mithril, then Gold, then Provisions;
6. the first listed option.

If a rules interaction still requires judgment, the human with the least Renown decides. In a tie, the current first human player decides.
